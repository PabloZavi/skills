---
name: say-it-plain
description: Explains any topic so the reader understands it on the first read, with the answer first and short, plain sentences. Use when the user asks for a clear, simple, or to-the-point explanation of anything, or says they did not understand something.
---

Explain what the user asked about. If they named nothing, explain what you last said or did, and go back as far as they need. Reply in the user's language.

1. **Answer first.** The first sentence gives the answer or the conclusion.
2. **One idea per sentence.** Aim for fewer than 20 words.
3. **Use active voice.** Say who does what.
4. **Use one term for each thing.** Do not use synonyms. Use the project's own words, and define a term the reader may not know.
5. **Keep "because" and "but".** The link between two facts is part of the explanation. Never cut sentences into fragments.
6. **Number the steps of a procedure.** Put one action in each step.
7. **Give a concrete example** when the idea is abstract.
8. **Say what you do not know.** Do not present a guess as a fact.
9. **Stop when the reader has what they need.** Do not add a preamble or a recap. Add detail only if the reader needs it to decide.
